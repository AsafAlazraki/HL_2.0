Every list sits on one scale against its own Cash, so what differs, what is undecided and what is Northside’s own read in one look, and a share is counted at the foot before it is written.

What Northside changes from its own settings

  • Which lists appear, and their order. The list’s own order is the default, never ranked by gap.
  • The Trade share, typed on the tick, with the census counted before it writes. And the switch.
  • The scale’s ends follow the file (55% is the lowest figure, 125.8% the highest), never a setting. The marks read the accent token.

Its silhouette, against what is built

  • Fitment as built plots one boat’s motors as dots on a horsepower axis inside a callout. E plots fourteen lists as rows on a percentage axis, with one dashed Cash line through all of them.
  • Prices as built is cells of words. E has no cells: a mark on a line, and words only at the right.
  • History’s spine is vertical nodes. E’s vertical is a reference line, and nothing hangs from it.

The five hard questions, answered here

  1. The matrix: each list is a row and each rung a mark. “Same as Cash” is a ring on the line, so most rows read alike at a glance, and only Highfield, Yamaha and the Not priced lists stand out.
  2. A missing rung is a ring on the Cash line: “Trade = Cash on 19 · 8 with none pay Cash”.
  3. Before a write, the dock reads 91 written · 91 equal Cash today · 0 already · 0 with no Cash · 309 Skimma $1,730 → $1,643, then one amber press. After it, the Stacer ring moves to 95% and the toast holds Undo, which puts all 91 back.
  4. Rung against Cash: position is the share, and the figures print under the ticks, so both are shown.
  5. The boundary: Warranty on Northside’s dark ground, on the same scale, with the Cash line running through both grounds.

Drawn from, and owed
