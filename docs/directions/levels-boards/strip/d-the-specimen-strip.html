What Northside changes from its own settings

  • Which line speaks for each list. By default it is the first priced line. It is a search parameter, so a link opens on the SP560.
  • The words for each buyer (“A trade buyer”), the Trade share, and whether Warranty may reach a customer.
  • The accent and the dark ground are tokens. The picture is the line’s own or nothing: with none, the name sets larger and no stand-in is drawn.

Its silhouette, against what is built

  • The picker shows one model as a photograph beside its specs. D has no specs: it is a stack of large figures with a change of ground under it.
  • Prices as built is a 14 × 5 matrix of words. D is one column of that matrix, at full size, with the matrix’s other columns as tabs.
  • Fitment centres a render inside callouts. D’s picture sits left at 456 px in a tinted panel, with no callouts.

The five hard questions, answered here

  1. The matrix: one list at a time. The tabs carry each list’s state, so the whole ladder reads across the top in one line: “One price · 91”, “Trade 95%”, “Not priced”.
  2. A missing rung reads its fallback with the figure: “A trade buyer pays Cash”, never an empty cell.
  3. Before a write, the share field counts, or refuses in place: “Every Highfield boat with a Cash figure already holds 95% of it as Trade.” After a write, there is one step in the toast with Undo.
  4. Rung against Cash: both figures, the share and the dollars: $45,932 · 95.0% of Cash · $2,418 less.
  5. The boundary is a change of ground under the customer rungs, with the switch at the head of Northside’s side.
