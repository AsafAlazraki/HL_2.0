The stage and the tray

The app’s own screens are the preview. Northside picks one thing at a time in a tray below them and sees it land on the door, Home, a quote or a place before anything is kept.

Order and grouping

By what is being changed, the look first: Colour, Pictures, Shape, Face and motion, Home’s order. Then Letterhead and People, which turn the stage to the quote. Everything else is one link at the tray’s right end.

What only this screen does

It is the one screen that draws the app’s other screens live, so one choice is seen landing on four surfaces without leaving the page.

Home, Northside’s to arrange

Home’s order is a tray tab: its sections as handles and eyes, drawn live on the stage. Home itself also offers Arrange, which opens this tray over Home.

What Northside can change about it

Everything the stage shows. Its colour (one in, ten steps out), the door’s photograph, Home’s hero, a place’s cover, corners, shadow, row density, face, motion and Home’s order. With the stage on A quote, its letterhead and its people.

How its silhouette differs from the built screens

The configurator is a dark stage with a priced rail at its right. This is a framed miniature of another screen on a dark floor, with a tabbed tray across the full width beneath it. No built screen has a tray for its lower half. Home is a sea band over tile rows; here Home appears only small, inside a frame.

Motion, keyboard, reduced motion

A press on a swatch re-sets one custom property on the stage’s scope, and the stage redraws in the same frame. Nothing animates and no figure counts. Switching surface cross-fades the miniature over 200 ms while the tray stays still. Keep raises one toast with Undo. Reduced motion: the cross-fade is a cut.

The surface switch and the tray’s tabs are two tablists, walked with the arrow keys. The swatches are a radio group. A refused swatch still takes focus and reads its sentence, and Enter on a nearest-pass chip chooses it.
