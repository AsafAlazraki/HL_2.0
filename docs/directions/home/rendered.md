# Home — what the four boards actually render

Screenshot pass: `npx tsx tools/research/shots.ts home`, 1440 × 900, deviceScaleFactor 1, all four boards clean — no page error, no failed request, nothing scrolling its own frame. Shots in `docs/directions/home/shots/`. Written by opening each PNG, not by reading the HTML.

---

## A — A shelf of kinds
`a-shelf-of-kinds.html` — 24,103 bytes (23.5 KB) · shot 503,632 bytes (492 KB)

A white screen in six horizontal bands: a 56px desk bar (wordmark, "Master Price File loaded", the search field with a `Ctrl K` keycap, and the one filled navy **New quote** at the far right), a greeting, the six-kind shelf, a full-bleed grey brand shelf, the drafts row, and an 11px provenance foot. The eye goes to the kind row first and it earns that: six 218 × 164 frames at exactly one size, three carrying a real picture (a Stacer on green water, a Yamaha F90 cut-out floating on white, a Stabicraft on a GFAB trailer against a mountain) and three carrying a pale "NO PICTURE" well that prints its own ratio — `0 OF 1,866 ROWS PICTURED`, `0 OF 3,587`, `0 OF 64`. That is the board's best moment: the absence is the same size and weight as the presence, so Parts & accessories at 3,587 rows does not get demoted for being unphotogenic, and no word anywhere sits on a photograph — every label is under its frame, on paper. Type is a system grotesk working hard at small sizes: a 30px two-tone headline ("Good morning." in ink, the rest in grey), 19px kind names, 12px counts, 11px provenance, and the provenance lines are where the density bites — three and four lines of 11px grey under each tile, and the grey right-hand note above the shelf is set ragged-right into a 560px block that reads as an afterthought hanging off the corner. The brand shelf below is the cleanest device on the board: seven photographs bottom-aligned on one 1px rule that bleeds past the 40px gutter, names beneath. Nothing is clipped, truncated or overlapping, and nothing is unreadable, but the shelf is not optically level — Highfield's provenance line wraps to two lines where the other six take one, so that column alone drops below the shared baseline. One substantive thing to notice rather than fix: this board pictures **all seven** boat makers by pulling catalogue frames from `/seed-images/`, where board D shows five of the seven as "not held" because it reads only the hero ledger. Both are true of the same file; the boards disagree about which shelf counts.

## B — Cinema day
`b-one-photograph.html` — 17,169 bytes (16.8 KB) · shot 991,824 bytes (969 KB)

One held photograph — the Highfield Adventure 7 running left-to-right with four people and a dog aboard, hills and open water behind — fills the entire 1440 × 900 under a thin navy top bar, with four opaque mattes pushed into the corners and the hull left completely alone in the middle third. It is the only board that looks like a photograph of a boat business rather than a photograph of a database, and the composition genuinely works: the boat is uncovered, the two top panels sit in the calm sky, the two bottom panels sit in the calm water, and the wake runs clear between them. The eye lands on the boat, then goes up-left to "Good morning." at ~34px in light weight, then right to the six figures. Those figures are the second-best thing here — 810 / 241 / 444 / 1,866 / 3,587 / 64 set large with 11px tracked caps beneath, three across and two down, with the joins sentence under a hairline. The amber **New quote** button is the only warm thing on the screen and it is correctly the loudest control; the white search field beside it is the only other bright object in the top-left panel. No text sits on the photograph anywhere — every panel is fully opaque — so nothing is unreadable and nothing is clipped. Two honest weaknesses. First, the bottom-right "Open drafts / 0" panel is warm white where the other three are navy, which makes the emptiest thing on the screen the brightest object in the frame and pulls the eye off the hull; the asymmetry reads as accidental rather than as emphasis. Second, the brand line in the bottom-left panel wraps badly — "Stacer 91 · Stabicraft 37 · Surtees 19 · Jeanneau 27" ends without its separator and "Haines Signature 9 · Highfield Inflatables 588 · Formosa 39" starts the second line, so the seven makers read as two ragged groups rather than one list, and the 11px provenance under it is the lowest-contrast type on the board. Both bottom panels also float ~40px clear of the window's bottom edge, leaving a strip of water under them that no other edge of the composition has.

## C — Rails of counts
`c-rails-of-counts.html` — 24,590 bytes (24.0 KB) · shot 179,400 bytes (175 KB)

Pure type on white with not one picture and not one mark — the lightest shot of the four by a wide margin, which is the direction's own argument made in bytes. Three rails under a 40px "Good morning." and a tracked-caps measured strip (`53 TABLES · 15,691 ROWS · 25 BASE REGISTERS HOLD 7,012 · 28 FITMENT JOINS HOLD 8,679`). The eye goes first to the headline, then immediately to the middle rail, because the seven boat makers each carry their row count in a boxed mono figure at the left — 91, 37, 19, 27, 9, 588, 39 — and those boxes are the only repeated geometry on the screen. The left rail is the strongest single element: six kinds, each with a right-aligned tabular count and a grey bar drawn to its true share of the 7,012 base rows, so Parts and rigging visibly owns half the rail and Labour, oils, registration is a stub you can barely see — the file's real shape, readable in one glance, with Boats marked active by a navy bar at the left edge rather than by a fill. Every row that cannot carry a price says so in words ("no price level declared", "Rigging Kits declares none") exactly where the price levels are named for the rows that can. Nothing is clipped, nothing overlaps, nothing is empty, and the board fits its frame — but only just: the right rail runs to y≈851 of 900 and is the densest column on any of the four, three stacked sections of 12–13px body text in a muted blue-grey that is the lowest-contrast sustained reading on the whole set. Two things to weigh. The right rail ends with a `DRAWN FROM` block citing `live/framework-marketplace.png` and `gallery/figma-community.png` and a note about wanting Söhne — that is board annotation printed inside the artboard, and it reads as part of the screen. And the two 11px paragraphs across the foot of the middle rail (cost columns, no from-price) are doing honest work but sit below the last Quote button as a wall of small grey text, which is where this board's density stops being confident and starts being a lot.

## D — The file, open
`d-the-file-open.html` — 17,767 bytes (17.4 KB) · shot 183,421 bytes (179 KB)

The calmest and most conventional of the four, and the clearest: a 56px header (wordmark, a 470px search field with `Ctrl K`, provenance right), then a 27px two-tone line — "Nothing of yours is open yet." in ink, "The file is." in grey — which is the single best sentence on any of the four boards and does the whole screen's job in seven words. Below it, two doors split by a 1px rule: YOUR WORK at 508px on the left, THE FILE in a tinted panel on the right. The eye goes to the headline, then right, because the panel has a ground and the left half does not. The right panel is the board's real content: a by-kind index with counts and table counts, then the seven makers as 132px-wide tiles — two carrying a real photograph (Stacer, Highfield) and **five** carrying a plain grey "not held" plate — each with joins and paired rows beneath and the row count right-aligned in large tabular figures with "rows" stacked under it. Five honest grey plates in a row is a strong, slightly bleak image, and it is the truth of the ledger. The left half is weaker: the card ("No draft exists yet.", four lines of explanation, a filled **New quote** and an underlined "or start from a boat") is good, and the five-row leadered list of what a card will carry is good, but the column runs out of content at about y≈690 and then there is roughly 85px of bare white before an 11px footnote, and more white below that — so the left door reads as unfinished against a right door that reaches the panel's bottom edge. Nothing is clipped, overlapping or unreadable; the only real flaw is that imbalance, plus the Stacer thumbnail, which is cropped so tightly at 132 × 72 that bow and stern are both cut and the boat is hard to read as a boat. The foot strip — 453 addresses, 329 held, 118 never fetched, 6 refused, seven tables with no price level, no marks held, and "Home has no total to show, so it shows none" — is the most complete statement of what the screen does not know on any of the four.
