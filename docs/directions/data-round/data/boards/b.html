Data · Direction B

Every list dated

The one job

The Master Price File as one place: what needs Northside, what is new, the rooms, and every list it carries, each a door (docs/SCREENS.md, the owner on 2026-10-31).

What only this screen does

It is the only screen that sets all 53 lists side by side, each with its own date, its own open work and its own source tab. The sheet reads one list. Review, Prices and Fitment each read one question across lists. Only Data answers “which list is behind, and in what?” in a single column.

Order and grouping

  1. 1. The title, with the six rooms as the page’s own counted tabs. The rooms come first, as a published dataset’s tabs do.
  2. 2. An About card. On the left is when: the large date, then packed, last change, and the oldest and newest prices a heading dates. Beside it is how big: lists, lines, and what a line is, split three ways. On the right is What needs Northside as a labelled table (label, sentence, door), with the new-here row last.
  3. 3. Every list: one table of 53. The 7 boat makers come first, then the 18 lists you sell from by kind, then the 28 lists of what fits, folded by maker. Every row carries its lines, what it fits, its oldest as-at date, the lines changed here, its Review cases, whether its price waits on Northside, and where in the workbook it came from.

The mark

Each maker’s mark stands at the start of its own row, 18 px tall in a 96 px box, so the seven boat rows line up as a column of marks. The marks appear in the table once, in the Boats group and in each maker’s fold of what fits. No plates and no cover. Stabicraft is set as a word.

What a dealer can replace

The accent (the date, the tab underline and the doors), the three inks of the split (the kind accents), and the density. A dealer who wants fewer columns hides “Where from” and “Held in” first, the same as the narrower widths do.

Motion

The tabs do not slide. The underline moves to the chosen tab over 160 ms, and the room opens as its own screen. Opening a maker’s fold of what fits draws its rows from height 0 over 200 ms. The rows below move down with them, the caret turns 90° over the same 200 ms, and the rest stays still. Pressing a row opens its lineup under the row (as drawn above), and the table scrolls to the row’s head, clear of the bar. Reduced motion: the fold and the lineup are simply there, and the caret swaps.

Keyboard

The tabs are one stop, with arrows inside. The needs table’s doors come in order. The table is a grid: arrows move between rows, Enter opens the sheet and Space opens the lineup. On a fold, Space opens and closes it. / focuses the find.

Honest constraints

  • “Priced as at” prints only where a heading says a date in its own words. 20 of the 25 lists you sell from and boat lists read “not dated”, and the 28 lists of what fits carry no headings to date. That is true. No date is inferred.
  • Review cases by list come from the Review reader’s own 37, split by the list each case is on (Parts 19, GFAB 4, Dunbier 3, Mackay 3, Rigging Kits 3, REDCO 2, Dealer Fit 2, Formosa 1).
  • “Changed here” is 0 on every row today, because every updatedAt equals packedAt. The column is kept because it is the column that moves first.
  • Cost and margin are not columns. “Price” says only whether the sale price waits on Northside.

Its risk

It is the densest of the three. The needs are a table rather than a list of work to finish, and at 390 a reader scrolls through 53 rows to reach the lists of what fits. Folding those lists by maker keeps the table to 32 rows until a maker is opened.

Owed whatever wins

The same three fixes as A: no sideways scroll on the OBSOLETE spread at 390, Make it refusing an empty name in a sentence, and a 72 px scroll margin under the bar at 844×390.
