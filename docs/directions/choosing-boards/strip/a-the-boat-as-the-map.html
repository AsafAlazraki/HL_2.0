The build’s one job

One chapter per decision with a live price, a search reaching every chapter, every refusal with its reason (docs/SCREENS.md, Configurator). This board draws chapter 04, Parts & fit-out, mid-way: three on, a fourth in the hand.

What only this direction does

It answers “what goes at the helm?” by pointing. The parts chapter opens on a side-on diagram of a boat in five places (Bow, Helm, Deck, Transom, Trailer); a place lists the aisles Northside put there, and what is on the quote stacks beside its place.

Against the original’s three floors

  • Configuring visually. The original showed the boat in a carousel and parts as blank cards. Here the parts have a place on a boat and their own pictures at one size in one box, and the Highfield’s finish puts the row’s own render on the stage.
  • A dealer’s own picture. The original had four admin screens, no record and an instant X. Here “Add a picture” is on the option in the build and the catalogue: drop, choose or take, “added by Asaf, 30 Oct 2026”, one item only, in the backup, undone by a command.
  • The catalogue. The original had 93 raw supplier headings and a counted chip. Here the 180 headings fold into 18 aisles, and the aisles into five places; everything not placed is counted, never hidden.

Motion, as choreography

Pick-up: the tile lifts 4px and takes the lift shadow in 120ms; its slot stays as a dashed ghost. Over a place the place fills with the drop wash in 90ms. On drop the tile shrinks into a chip beside its place over 220ms and the place’s count ticks; the tray grows one item from the right. Nothing else on the page moves. Reduced motion: no travel; the shadow, the wash and the count change in place.

Keyboard

Tab to a tile, Space lifts, ← → step place to place (each announced: “Over the helm”), Space drops, Esc returns it. The tile’s menu offers “Add to the quote, at the helm”, which is the drop as one press. The five places are a row of buttons under the diagram, reachable in order.

The dropdown the walk named, replaced

“Show all 2,238 in Parts & Accessories” (a disclosure showing the first 40 as text) becomes five places, each with its aisles counted. “Show all 622 in Rigging Kits” goes: the kit for this quote’s motor sits in Steering, controls & rigging.

What Northside can replace

Where each aisle goes (its one new setting), its own picture on any part, the accent and act colours. The diagram is the app’s, never a model’s.

The honest constraints

  • The price list lists 1–10 parts per boat and none pictured: the 529 Assault Pro’s four (Battery MFM50 $199, Battery Box · Small $18, Battery Terminals · 1 pair $18, D Shackle · 10 mm Galv $3) all draw the empty square.
  • 197 of 2,238 parts have the maker’s own picture; the radios shown are among them. Prices are the Cash level (parts.l); no cost anywhere.
  • The diagram claims no model, and is never tinted a colourway.
  • The places are drawn as if set. On day one every aisle is “not placed yet”: the cost of this direction.
  • The rigging kit for the F115LB has no chosen price; it says so and links to Prices.

New quote, in this direction

“What is being sold?” A boat (the largest door, with makers’ marks), a motor on its own, a trailer on its own, parts & fit-out on their own. A parts-only quote opens this same map with no boat chosen.

Risk

One new setting (each aisle’s place), and an outline that suits a runabout better than a Highfield RIB.
