The showpiece, live on the board

The name rises out of the water a letter at a time (35 ms apart, a 700 ms rise from a 6 px blur), the greeting follows at 200 ms and the act settles on the spring at 520 ms; the capsules’ counts roll into place as NumberFlow lands them. It plays once a session, at the door, and never on a return to Home, which a salesperson sees a hundred times a day.

Bolder tech, and why it earns its place

The WebGL water (src/ui/water.ts, about 2 KB, no library) is the band’s ground: its luminance is capped at the accent’s own, so the white name reads at 5.8 : 1 or better on the brightest ripple; it stops while a caret is in a field and under reduced motion. A View Transition carries a maker’s mark from its tile into the picker’s head (named, not drawn here). NumberFlow on the capsules, which are counts and never a price.

Day one

Nothing is open, given or filed, so the capsules and the rail are not drawn at all: one sentence says so, and the makers take the room, ten doors at full size under the name. Nothing on day one is a lesson or a well.

How it reflows

The phone order is the order of the jobs: the act second, then the week, then the makers. 1280 × 800 is the board itself with the band 24 px shorter. Nothing floats at the foot: the pill stands at the head on a phone too.

Its silhouette, against the screens beside it

Its nearest relative is Data, which also opens on makers’ marks. Data’s are a strip of seven small cards that open the price list; A’s are three times the size, ten of them, Yamaha among them, and each opens the picker with the maker’s boats and pictures. Quotes is a list beside a panel and the sheet is a grid: A is the only screen whose body is doors.
