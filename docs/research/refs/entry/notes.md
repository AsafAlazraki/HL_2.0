## 2. Patterns worth taking, named

**Two doors, each explained.** Five instances, and they differ in *how* the second door is explained, which is the choice a board has to make: B&O (`live/bo-login.png`) uses three check-marked lines; Herman Miller (`live/hermanmiller-login.png`) gives each door a headline, a sentence and a black button ("Visit Account Page" / "Check Your Order Status" — two different jobs, not the same job twice); Marriott (`live2/marriott-signin.png`) uses a row of four icon-over-caption benefits; Apple (`live/apple-signin.png`) hangs a qualifying line under the second button; **Lotus** (`live2/lotus-my.png`) stacks both doors down one column, each with its own head, sentence and button, so the picture keeps the other half of the window. Taken here: the file door and the blank door are peers in composition, each carrying a sentence, with the primary said by fill alone rather than by size.

**The honesty line.** Excalidraw (`gallery/excalidraw-blank.png`) and Coda (`gallery/coda-login.png`) both put a plain sentence at body size exactly where the password would have been; Coda's sits in a tinted band — "We'll email you a link for a password-free sign in. Or sign in with a password." Our M6 sentence goes there, at body size, once, never in a tooltip.

**Tenant with the rest supplied.** Slack (`gallery/slack-workspace.png`) shows the fixed part of the answer inside the field so only the variable part is typed. For us "Northside Marine" is not typed at all — it is stated, and the field takes only the name at the desk.

**Choose a method.** Deel's card is literally subtitled "Choose a method to log in" (`gallery/deel-login.png`); Tines paints three pills and no input at all on first paint (`gallery/tines-login.png`); Linear the same with four (`live/linear-login.png`). The lesson is the count: at three or more the eye reads a list, at two it makes a choice.

**Two buttons, one row, two audiences.** Mercury (`live/mercury-mercnet.png`) and Apple (`live/apple-signin.png`) both end on one row of two equal-width buttons, and Apple's second carries a qualifying line beneath it ("Requires a device with iOS 17 or later") — where ours says what the blank sheet does not load.

**The product behind the door.** folk (`gallery/folk-login.png`) tilts a real screenshot on warm paper; Clay (`gallery/clay-login.png`) crops a real table with a real row count. Honest for us only if the picture is the seed's own sheet or a real frozen quote, never a mock.

**Photograph as ground, card as door.** Two real instances, both re-read for this round: Lucid (`live/lucid-portal-login.png`) sets an **opaque** 480 px black card on a coastal aerial, and Garmin (`live/garmin-signin.png`) floats a 464 px white one on a lifestyle photograph. In neither is the picture veiled — Lucid's only scrim is a short gradient under the nav. (Headspace, `gallery/headspace-login.png`, was cited here as "a sky photograph". It is not: opened again, it is a flat blue gradient with soft cloud shapes and no photographic detail. Cat Cay, `gallery/catcay-home.png`, is a frame grab from a video loop — see §3.)

**Ground with no card at all.** Where the picture has a quiet region, the type goes straight on it: Zodiac (`live2/zodiac-home.png`) drops the caption into the empty lower-left quadrant behind a short blue rule; Princess (`live2/princess-home.png`) uses water with nothing in it and puts one pill low; Stabicraft (`live2/stabicraft-home.png`) sets one white box into the sea. The counter-example is Quintrex (`live2/quintrex-home.png`), which lays a translucent white panel **over the boat** so the headline crosses the hull — a legibility patch applied to a picture that was never chosen for type.

**The mark as an object.** Riviera hangs its pennant off the top edge across the nav rule (`…\ref\entry\riviera-owners.png`); Apple rings its mark in coloured dots (`live/apple-signin.png`); Contra photographs its mark as eight glass wedges (`gallery/contra-login.png`). Three answers to "I want the logo to be the showpiece", none of them an icon in a corner. **Which mark is a live question, not a given** — `public/brand-marks/` and `marks-ledger.json` hold twelve suppliers' marks with provenance but none of Northside Marine's own. See §6.

**The alternatives inside the sentence.** Netlify Drop (`live2/netlify-drop.png`) makes the primary act the whole window and writes the other two ways in as links inside one line of body text: "or choose a **file**, **folder**, or **start from an example**." A second door does not have to be a second button competing with the first.

**Verb, not noun, on the button.** Frame.io says "Let's go" (`gallery/frame-login.png`). Ours say what happens: "Load the Master Price File", "Start a blank sheet".

## 4. What to avoid, each with its frame

- **An error before a keystroke** — `live/bo-login.png`: "The email address is a mandatory field" in red under an untouched field.
- **A silent grey submit** — stock `bmw-login.png`: "Register now" flat grey behind five password rules each led by a ✕, before a letter is typed. If the manifest is missing, the file door stays a named button that explains itself when pressed.

- **Three or more equal doors** — `live/linear-login.png` (four), `live/notion-login.png` (six tiles), `gallery/loom-login.png` (five stacked).
