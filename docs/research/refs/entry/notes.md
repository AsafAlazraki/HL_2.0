## 2. Patterns worth taking, named

**Two doors, each explained.** B&O (`live/bo-login.png`) and Herman Miller (`live/hermanmiller-login.png`) both set two doors side by side, each with its own headline, sentence and button; Herman Miller's are "Visit Account Page" and "Check Your Order Status" — two different jobs, not the same job twice. Taken here: the file door and the blank door are peers in composition, each carrying a sentence, with the primary said by fill alone rather than by size.

**The honesty line.** Excalidraw (`gallery/excalidraw-blank.png`) and Coda (`gallery/coda-login.png`) both put a plain sentence at body size exactly where the password would have been; Coda's sits in a tinted band — "We'll email you a link for a password-free sign in. Or sign in with a password." Our M6 sentence goes there, at body size, once, never in a tooltip.

**Tenant with the rest supplied.** Slack (`gallery/slack-workspace.png`) shows the fixed part of the answer inside the field so only the variable part is typed. For us "Northside Marine" is not typed at all — it is stated, and the field takes only the name at the desk.

**Choose a method.** Deel's card is literally subtitled "Choose a method to log in" (`gallery/deel-login.png`); Tines paints three pills and no input at all on first paint (`gallery/tines-login.png`); Linear the same with four (`live/linear-login.png`). The lesson is the count: at three or more the eye reads a list, at two it makes a choice.

**Two buttons, one row, two audiences.** Mercury (`live/mercury-mercnet.png`) and Apple (`live/apple-signin.png`) both end on one row of two equal-width buttons, and Apple's second carries a qualifying line beneath it ("Requires a device with iOS 17 or later") — where ours says what the blank sheet does not load.

**The product behind the door.** folk (`gallery/folk-login.png`) tilts a real screenshot on warm paper; Clay (`gallery/clay-login.png`) crops a real table with a real row count. Honest for us only if the picture is the seed's own sheet or a real frozen quote, never a mock.

**Photograph as ground, card as door.** Headspace (`gallery/headspace-login.png`) fills the window with sky and floats one 32 px-radius card; Cat Cay (`gallery/catcay-home.png`) fills it with an aerial and makes the door a single navy pill. Nimbus's 404 (`…\ref\entry\nimbus-account.png`) proves the cheaper version: two white boxes set straight on a dark photograph, no card, still legible.

**The mark as an object.** Riviera hangs its pennant off the top edge across the nav rule (`…\ref\entry\riviera-owners.png`); Apple rings its mark in coloured dots (`live/apple-signin.png`); Contra photographs its mark as eight glass wedges (`gallery/contra-login.png`). **Verb, not noun, on the button.** Frame.io says "Let's go" (`gallery/frame-login.png`). Ours say what happens: "Load the Master Price File", "Start a blank sheet".

## 6. The imagery question, measured

Counted from `data/northside/images.json` and `docs/data/IMAGES.md`, not estimated: **453 addresses · 329 held · 122 scenes · 207 studio**. Highfield: **212 addresses, 115 held, 0 scenes, 115 studio** — the plan's "renders only" is now a measured fact. And **83 of the 122 scenes came off the dealership's own mirror of northsidemarine.com.au**, so the dealer's own photography, not the manufacturers', is the largest source of real water in the file.

The Stacer 529 Assault Pro does have on-water photography: `public/seed-images/529-assault-lifestyle-tiffs-7-1024x676-fbf66995.webp`, verdict `scene`, **1024 × 676**, held off the dealer's mirror of `northsidemarine.com.au/stacer-boats/…/529-Assault-Lifestyle-Tiffs-7-1024x676.jpg`. Note the size: the packer caps every held copy at long edge 1100 (`images.json` → `meta.longEdge`), and only 39 held scenes are 1100 px wide at all. **No held picture can fill a 1440 × 900 window without upscaling.** So either `tools/seed/fetch-images.ts` re-fetches the entry's chosen hero at a larger long edge, or the photographic directions are drawn at a size the bytes actually carry — which is precisely what Rivian's crop (`live/rivian-login.png`) and Frame.io's one-third column (`gallery/frame-login.png`) do, and why both sit on board A.

What the best sites do when they have only renders — the Highfield case: they stop pretending there is a photograph. Nimbus replaces it with a colour field (`live/nimbus-connect.png`); Saxdor uses a ghosted wordmark as the ground with navy and gold as the whole palette (stock `saxdor-login.png`); Apple makes the mark itself the picture (`live/apple-signin.png`); Contra photographs its own mark in glass (`gallery/contra-login.png`); Riviera hangs a pennant off the frame (`…\ref\entry\riviera-owners.png`). None substitutes another model's photograph — which the image ledger forbids anyway. A Highfield-drawn direction therefore finds its sense of place in the mark and two colours, and says so on the board.
