## 5. Directions that cannot be drawn on the ported engine as written

**history A and B — the two-calendars fault, at home.** `groupByDay(quotes)` cuts the list by
`localDay(q.createdAt)`. A quote started on Monday and issued on Friday sits under Monday, with
`20260824-01 is issued` among the sentences beneath it — a head from one calendar over events from
another, which is what §0.1 caught GitHub doing. A ("one folded line per quote touched that day")
and B ("that day's quotes") need an index of events by day, which is a pure function of
`QuoteDef.events[].at` and cheap, but it is not in `diary/`, and the sweep should name it as the
hole it is, the way cascade named `stays` and the declined name. B's gutter tally `$66,584 given`
is a sum of `quoteTotals` over the day's given quotes — derivable — while §0 says the engine's
tally "is only ever a length"; both sentences are in the notes.

**customers A — the sort the contract refuses.** `readCustomers`: "Rows are never re-sorted here:
the sheet's order is the dealer's order, and a list that silently alphabetises is a list that
disagrees with the table it came from." `matchCustomers` keeps register order behind its two rungs.
A is "a narrow alphabetised list, letters as section heads". Drawable — a sort is a sort — but A
disagrees with the sheet its own rows live in, and with customers B, which is that sheet. The board
must say the sort out loud, as a control the dealer pressed, and keep register order as the rest
state, or A contradicts the engine's own reason for existing.

**sheet D — a level the pack does not declare.** `buildGroups(rows, levelIds, textOf)` takes
explicit level ids, so D's master list on `Highfield × Yamaha` is `buildGroups(rows, [boatFieldId])`
with `leafCount` as the `3 motors` — drawable. The sweep says the join has "no hierarchy" and never
says D hands the grid one. Say it.

**sheet C — Compare is nobody's.** Three records as columns with a chooser at each head exists
nowhere in `table/core` or `sections.ts`. A pure derivation, un-named.

**customers C — the miniature.** "A miniature of its own A4 cover from the document screen's
render" is the document's DOM scaled, never a second renderer (the document's own rule, and its
reason for `@page`). The board should say which.

**data — every board is a reading of the manifest.** Nothing here needs an engine that does not
exist. `NORTHSIDE_MODULES`, `TABLE_KINDS`, `description`, `sourceSha256` all carry what the boards print.

## Which sweep is weakest, and the one thing that would most improve it

**Weakest: `sheet`.**

Its §0 is the strongest engine reading in the round — 588 rows in 8 sections under `series ▸ model
▸ variant`, the 50-token Variant column, `Recommended` true on exactly 588 join rows, the engine's
own heights, and the honest arithmetic that an open two-level sheet reads 17.6 rows against the 18 it
owes. It is weakest on completeness:

1. It cites **none** of `dense-tables-and-selection.md`, the document written for this screen,
   while re-buying four of its findings from fresh frames and missing three of its rules outright,
   one of which (150 px versus the engine's 116) is a live disagreement.
2. Two of its four directions rest on exclusives that show **no shape**: B on a help page whose
   gallery is a 40 px strip, D on two relationship diagrams — one of them printing the
   *One-to-Many / Many-to-Many* vocabulary the brief forbids a dealer to meet.
3. C and D share Grist, and B's Highfield page is a site a built screen and another M2 board
   already claim.
4. Its ledger holds the frames that would fix 2 — `tools/nocodb-gallery`,
   `tools/airtable-gallery-collection`, `tools/grist-table` — marked **NOT OPENED**.

**The one thing: read `dense-tables-and-selection.md` against `helpers.ts` and write the paragraph
where the published numbers and the ported engine disagree** — 150 px floor against 116, 32 px
default row against `ROW_H = 28`, "fixed layout above twelve columns" against FIT, WCAG 2.1.4
against `keys.ts`'s single-key vocabulary — then re-source B and D from the three unopened frames
so each board has a picture of its own shape. That single pass turns the sheet sweep from a
re-derivation into the reconciliation the plan's UI-kit rule asks for ("the 18-row density
requirement is stated per table shape … before the grid is written"), and it is the only sweep of
the five where the old research and the new engine can be checked against each other line by line.

**Runner-up: `shell`**, for a different reason — not incompleteness but two boards that fail the
owner's data-door instruction and one that is its own §4's first avoid. Its one thing: redraw D as
the one-line Cockpit bar it already describes, on every screen, with Data on it, and say what B's
rail does to Home's two-photograph fold in pixels.

**Ranked, for what it is worth:** customers and data strongest (customers for reading the contract
into every hard question and the best duplicate-handling section in the repo; data for a census that
reproduces to the row and an imagery answer that says "none of it" and means it), history next (the
best single catch of the round and the best engine reading, undone by A/B on the wrong calendar and
the Linear pair), shell next, sheet last.
